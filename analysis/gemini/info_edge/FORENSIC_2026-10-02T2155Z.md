# Forensic reconciliation — BTC5m window 17:50–17:55 ET (21:50–21:55 UTC), 2026-10-02

Prompted by a Gemini UI screenshot at ~17:54:55 ET. Collection was **not
disturbed**; the corrected collector kept running throughout. No strategy rule
was changed. No live orders. Sealed OOS untouched.

**Naming correction:** the index is **`LS-LRRR-XBTUSD`** (LRRR), not
`LS-LRHR`. The API and the screenshot both say LRRR. This matters for
endpoint hunting.

## HEADLINE

**The UI's "Up 51%" was NOT a price at which UP could be bought.** At that
moment the book had **no ask at all** — UP was unbuyable at any price. The 51
was the **208-second-stale last trade**, which happened to equal the best
**bid**, i.e. the price at which UP could be *sold*. UP then won.

This was **UI staleness on the probability display, not a capturable
mispricing.**

## 2. WHAT "Up 51% / Down 49%" REPRESENTS — RECONCILED, NOT INFERRED

The REST `prices` object polled at `21:54:49.318`:

```json
{"buy":{"yes":"0.51","no":"0.16"}, "sell":{"yes":"0.84","no":"0.49"},
 "bestBid":"0.84", "lastTradePrice":"0.51"}
```

- **`lastTradePrice` = 0.51**, from the 21:51:31 trade — **203 s stale**.
- **`buy.yes` = 0.51** and **`sell.no` = 0.49**, and **51 + 49 = 100 exactly**.
- Executable prices can **never** sum to 100: by the identity verified in this
  lane, acquiring both sides costs `1 + spread` (taker) or `1 − spread`
  (maker), never exactly 1. **A pair summing to exactly 100 is therefore a
  complementary pair derived from ONE number, not two tradeable quotes.**

**Verdict: the UI's 51/49 is the last trade price and its complement.** It
coincided with the best bid (0.51) purely because the last trade had been a
bid-side print. It is **not** an executable acquisition price, and on this
occasion the acquisition price for UP did not exist.

**One unresolved inconsistency, flagged rather than explained away:** the REST
`prices.bestBid` reads **0.84** while the websocket book's best bid is
**0.51**, and `sell.yes` = 0.84 agrees with the REST field, not the book.
These two Gemini surfaces disagree at the same instant. I have not established
which is authoritative; **the websocket book is what this lane trades off, and
the REST `prices` object should not be used for execution decisions.**

### The four questions, answered

1. **Was an UP order around 51¢ executable at ~5 s TTE?**
   **No.** There was no ask. UP could not be bought at 51¢ or any other price.
2. **Quantity available if it was?**
   **Zero for buying UP.** The only executable action was *selling* UP (= buying
   DOWN at 0.49) against **1.96 contracts**.
3. **Could a resting 51¢ maker buy have defensibly filled?**
   **No.** It would have joined **behind 1.96 contracts already at 0.51**, and
   **zero trades occurred in the final 208 s**. Under the corrected FIFO rule
   (through-price fills regardless of queue; same-price only once observed
   traded volume exceeds the queue joined behind) there is **no fill**.
4. **UI lag or real mispricing?**
   **UI staleness on the probability, and an absent offer — not a capturable
   mispricing.** The UI's *index* display was accurate (it showed +$22.77;
   settlement came in at +$21.05). The *51%* was a 203-second-old last trade.
   The market was simply not offering UP to anyone.

**Had the UI number been tradeable, it would have been profitable** — UP won,
and 0.51 → 1.00 is +0.49/contract. That is precisely why the distinction
matters: **the apparent edge lives entirely in a number that could not be
transacted.**

## 5. COINBASE vs INDEX — the genuinely interesting residue

At 21:54:55 the UI index read **84,521.95** (+22.77) while Coinbase read
**84,514.83** (+15.65): **Coinbase was ~$7.1 BELOW the index.** By 21:54:59
Coinbase had fallen to 84,500.28 (+1.10) while the index settled at
84,520.23 (+21.05).

That pattern is consistent with **LRRR being a smoothed/trailing rate** —
Coinbase moved down late while the index, averaging, did not follow. **This is
exactly the direction that makes the lead/lag question worth asking, and it
also warns that Coinbase can diverge sharply from settlement truth precisely
when it matters most.**
