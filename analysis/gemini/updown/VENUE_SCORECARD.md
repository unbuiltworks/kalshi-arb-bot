## Findings

1. **Pair-cost compression is the discriminating dimension, and it separates Polymarket from everything else.** L5's realized sequential VWAP pair cost has median **0.977**, with **59.64% (n=223)** of its complemented events below $1.00 and **43.95% (n=223)** at or below $0.96 (n=223 complemented events of 312, 2026-08-30). Gemini BTC5M's simultaneous pair cost is **never** below $1.00 across 24,520 observations (min 1.01, median 1.24), and that result is NOT independently reproduced (see the caveat below: `policy_d_port_ext` carries the UP leg only and cannot speak to pair cost). Kalshi's 15-minute series are far tighter than Gemini (median 1.02, min 1.001) but also never below $1.00 in the 2,269-row sample.
   The comparison is not like-for-like in kind: L5's figure is a *sequential realized* VWAP across a window, the Gemini/Kalshi figures are *simultaneous book* costs. A patient sequential strategy can beat a simultaneous snapshot. But Gemini's simultaneous floor of 1.01 with a **median 1.24** leaves no room for sequential patience to recover 24 cents, and only ~4 prints per 5-minute window to work with.

2. **Gemini's binding constraint is not depth, it is price.** Touch depth is adequate (median 500 contracts on BTC15M; both sides executable at q5 68.2%). The problem is that the two legs never sum below par, and the tape is the thinnest of any candidate (median 4 prints per 300s window vs L5's own 12.6 fills/minute).

3. **Kalshi is the strongest non-Polymarket candidate, and the only one with sourced maker economics.** `analysis/kalshi/venue_census.json` (observed 2026-09-09, 183,329 programs scanned, 2,790 active) carries published liquidity-reward terms - reward_usd and target_size per series - and 83.1% of the leg-tape rows were captured while a program was active. Window length is 900s, matching L5 exactly. Per CLAUDE.md this is **sourced program terms, not credited economics**: no reward may be assumed until actually credited and source-accounted.
   Kalshi's gap is behavioural observability: prints are anonymous, so per-actor OPEN/EXTEND/COMPLETE sequences, complement reach and first-inventory-to-complement lag are **UNAVAILABLE**. Kalshi cannot currently be validated against the action-by-action standard.

4. **Polymarket sports permits cycling but on the wrong clock, and its fee metadata is a red flag.** The 47,120-print census over 71 events (2026-08-18 to 2026-09-02) shows real per-actor two-sided behaviour, but the events are multi-day rather than 300/900s windows, which breaks L5's window-anchored cadence outright. Separately, every one of the 71 events carries `maker_fee_bps=1000` / `taker_fee_bps=1000` in its metadata. That is **reported, not confirmed as effective**. If it is the real charged rate, 10% per side destroys any pair-cycling economics and this environment is disqualified. Confirming the effective rate is a prerequisite to ranking it at all.

5. **PMUS's 0.0050 spread is an artifact and must not be used as a headline.** See the `POLYMARKET_SPORTS_PMUS_BBO` section: 232 snapshots of **2** markets over **12 minutes**, one of which is quote-frozen for the whole sample, with every bid on an exact 0.0050 grid - so 0.0050 is one minimum tick with the quote at the touch, not a distribution. Worse for the purpose: `ask_qty` is null in 232/232 rows, so *both-sides executability cannot be computed at all*, and the impressive ~167k-share median bid depth is one-sided. This file cannot support a venue decision.

6. **Adverse selection is UNAVAILABLE for every environment.** No corpus on disk aligns own fills with contemporaneous book state and subsequent mark. It is not estimated here. It is a genuine gap in the ranking and the highest-value next measurement, because it is the dimension most likely to reverse an apparent maker edge on Kalshi.

## What this implies for deployment venue

Gemini is **not** established as the right deployment venue by this scorecard, and nothing here protects it. Its one genuine structural match is window length (300s/900s short windows); on the decisive dimension - pair-cost compression - it is the worst-performing environment measured, by a wide margin, on the largest sample available (24,520 observations). That result rests on the single frozen census and is **not** independently reproduced: the second Gemini corpus on disk (`policy_d_port_ext`, 45,115 BBO rows) turns out to carry the UP leg only, with zero DOWN-leg rows, so it cannot speak to pair cost at all. Independent confirmation of Gemini's pair-cost floor is therefore still outstanding, and that is a caveat against the conclusion, not for it.

The ordering the evidence supports:

- **Polymarket crypto up/down** remains the only environment where L5-like pair-cost compression is *demonstrated* rather than hoped for - because that is where it was demonstrated, by L5. Its book/depth/spread structure is UNAVAILABLE on disk, which is the gap to close before it can be treated as a deployment target rather than a reference.
- **Kalshi 15-minute series** is the best-evidenced *executable* alternative: 900s windows, pair cost a full 22 cents tighter than Gemini at the median, two-sided depth in the low thousands of contracts, and the only sourced maker-reward programs. Blocked on behavioural observability and on a sample only ~83 minutes long for the two-sided measurements.
- **Gemini short-window** is retained only as the incumbent plumbing, not as a structural fit.
- **Polymarket sports / PMUS** is parked: wrong clock, unconfirmed 1000bps fee metadata, and a BBO sample too small and too one-sided to score.

Next measurements, in priority order: (a) confirm the effective Polymarket sports fee rate; (b) capture a synchronous two-sided Polymarket crypto up/down book so the reference environment can be scored on the same dimensions as the candidates; (c) extend the Kalshi leg tape well beyond 83 minutes; (d) build any corpus at all that permits an adverse-selection measurement.
