# Ladder coordinate system — results

Frozen protocol **c65bf29f**. 1,805 level-inference events, 899 new levels,
12 BTC15m windows, maker fills, **bids only**. S3 untouched and frozen at
`8b20a259…87df`. No live orders. No next-action model built.

## Answer to the central question

> **Can the 5–15-level lower-bound ladder be generated by a small stable set
> of offsets from the moving touch?**

**Yes — by essentially ONE offset: the touch itself.** But not in the way
the question's framing implies, and three of the four preregistered bars
fail. The ladder is **not** a set of simultaneously-posted distinct depths.

### The decisive measurement

| level number | n | median offset from touch | mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 656 | **0 ticks** | −0.17 |
| 2 | 397 | **0 ticks** | +0.51 |
| 3 | 232 | **0 ticks** | +0.53 |
| 4 | 154 | **0 ticks** | +0.90 |
| 5 | 106 | **0 ticks** | +0.57 |
| 6 | 73 | **0 ticks** | +0.34 |
| 7 | 52 | +1 tick | +1.71 |
| 8 | 37 | **0 ticks** | +1.16 |

`rho(level_number, offset_ticks) = **0.138**`

**Level number does not predict depth.** Every rank, from the nearest to
the eighth, sits at the touch when it first becomes inferable. A genuine
multi-depth ladder requires deeper *rank* to mean deeper *offset*; it does
not.

### The reconciliation

Yet when ≥5 levels are live at once, the span of their offsets from the
**current** touch is median **21 ticks** (IQR 10–37).

Both facts are true at once, and only one model explains that:

> **New exposure appears AT the touch. The apparent multi-level ladder is
> the accumulated trail of past at-the-touch exposures, left behind as the
> touch moves away from them.**

The "ladder" is a footprint, not a structure. Its width is a record of how
far the touch has wandered, not a design parameter.

### What this unifies

This single coordinate system retro-explains four earlier results that each
looked separate:

| earlier result | explanation |
| --- | --- |
| the ~0.06 "displacement bound" was a **FILL-LOCATION STATISTIC**, scaling with touch travel (rho +0.44) | it measures how far the touch wandered before returning to old levels, not a ladder depth |
| **no spacing model wins** (below) | spacing is not designed; it is the touch's own random walk |
| **T_RECENTER fails 0/12** | levels are not re-posted at a constant offset — they are created at the touch and abandoned |
| ladder span median **$0.63** but envelope width median **$0.07** | the $0.07 envelope is live at-touch exposure; the $0.63 span is the whole window's trail |

## Preregistered bars — three of four fail

| test | bar | result | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **spacing models** | ≥20 % better + ≥9/12 wins | M4 best, **11.7 %**, 6/12 | **NOT_DISCRIMINATED** |
| **T_STABLE** | IQR ≤ 2 ticks AND ≥9/12 windows | IQR **3 ticks**, 12/12 windows | **FAIL** (on IQR only) |
| **T_SET** | smallest set covering ≥80 % | **{−3…+4} ticks**, 80.1 % | reported |
| **T_RECENTER** | median \|Δoffset\| ≤1 tick in ≥9/12 | median **2 ticks**, **0/12** | **FAIL** |

### Spacing models

| model | LOWO median L1 (ticks) | held-out wins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **M4** contract-volatility-scaled | **2.239** | 6 |
| **M1** uniform (ticks ≡ cents) | 2.536 | 3 |
| M2 increasing with depth | 2.538 | 2 |
| M3 probability-scaled | 2.930 | 1 |

**NOT_DISCRIMINATED.** M4 leads but misses both halves of the bar. Pooled
consecutive gaps: median **2 ticks**, p75 4, p90 8, max 57.

Per the frozen degeneracy note, **M1's result is ONE result, not two** —
the Polymarket grid *is* $0.01, so uniform-ticks and uniform-cents are the
same model.

M4's lead is worth one line of caution: it scales spacing by **contract-price**
volatility, and the gaps it predicts are partly the same random walk that
generated the levels, so a modest edge there is close to self-prediction.

### T_STABLE — fails narrowly, and the failure is informative

IQR is **3 ticks** against a ≤2 bar. But **all 12 windows** have a median
first-inference offset of **0 or 1 tick**:

`0, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0`

So the *central tendency* is as stable as it could be; only the per-level
dispersion is a tick wider than the frozen bar allows. I am not moving the
bar. The honest reading: **the offset is centred at the touch with ±2–3
ticks of jitter**, and the jitter is slightly too wide to call the set
"tight" under the preregistered criterion.

## Distributions

| | pooled |
| --- | --- |
| offset from touch at first inference | median **0 ticks**, IQR −1…+2, p10 −3, p90 +4 |
| concurrent live levels (**LOWER BOUND**) | median **4**, p75 7, p90 10, max **15** |
| latent quantity (**LOWER BOUND**) | median ~**23–30 shares** by rank, broadly flat |
| lifetime (**LOWER BOUND**) | median **0 s**, p90 1–3 s |
| **P(full consumption to 80 \| inferable)** | **0.185** overall |

`P(full consumption)` declines with rank — 0.21, 0.18, 0.20, 0.21, 0.14,
0.11, 0.10, 0.05 — so levels ranked deeper at their moment of inference are
less likely to be emptied.

**The selection tautology, as preregistered:** `P(any consumption |
inferable) ≡ 1.0` by construction, because a level is only visible through
its fills. Only the 80-ceiling version above is reported, and it is never
called "probability of consumption".

Negative offsets (p10 = −3 ticks) mean exposure **inside or above** the
touch, which is consistent with the earlier §5 finding that 28 % of clips
start at or above the touch.

## One correction made during the run

`n_live_levels_lower_bound` first came out at median **1**, max 5 —
contradicting the 5–15 bound from the ladder study. Cause: I measured
liveness with **per-clip** spans, whose median length is **0 s**, instead of
the **per-price** spans (first…last fill at a price, across all its clips)
that the Q2 interval-overlap bound used. Corrected to per-price spans, it
reads median **4**, max **15** — consistent with Q2. The spacing-gap
computation used the same corrected liveness.

## Evidence tiering

### DIRECTLY OBSERVED
- Median offset from touch at first inference is **0 ticks at every level
  rank 1–8**; `rho(rank, offset) = 0.138`.
- When ≥5 levels are live, their offsets span median **21 ticks** from the
  current touch.
- Every one of 12 windows has a median first-inference offset of 0 or 1 tick.
- Pooled consecutive gaps: median 2 ticks, p90 8.
- `P(full consumption to 80 | inferable) = 0.185`, declining with rank.
- Concurrent live levels: median 4, max 15 — **a lower bound**.

### STRONGLY INFERRED
- **New exposure is placed at the touch, not at designed depths.** The
  multi-level ladder is the trail of past at-touch exposure as the touch
  moves away.
- **The ladder has no stable spacing rule.** No spacing model clears the
  bar, and the gap distribution is what a wandering touch produces.
- **Levels are not recentered at a constant offset** (T_RECENTER 0/12);
  they are created at the touch and left behind.
- The coordinate system is **one offset (≈0 ticks) plus the touch's own
  path** — not a set of offsets.

### POSSIBLE
- Spacing may scale weakly with contract-price volatility (M4 leads by
  11.7 %), but this is partly self-prediction and misses the bar.
- The ±2–3 tick jitter around the touch may be venue tick-rounding, queue
  competition, or genuine spread, which this data cannot separate.

### UNIDENTIFIABLE
- **True exposed quantities and real ladder width.** Cancels and unfilled
  orders leave no trace; every quantity and count here is a floor, and no
  unfilled order was inferred.
- **Whether old levels were cancelled or left resting.** "Abandoned" means
  no further fills were observed, not that an order was withdrawn — the
  distinction is invisible.
- **Actual order lifetimes.** Median observed lifetime is 0 s, which bounds
  nothing from below.
- The exact strike / settlement index (G-STRIKE failed 10/12); nothing here
  uses one, and M4 uses **contract-price** volatility only.

## The coordinate system, as asked

Rather than another next-action model:

```
place at:        touch + jitter,  jitter ∈ [−3, +4] ticks   (80 % coverage,
                                   median 0, IQR −1…+2)
quantity:        latent clip, ≤80 ceiling, median ~25 shares  (LOWER BOUND)
both outcomes:   yes
what follows:    nothing is repositioned. The touch moves; the old level is
                 left where it was and is hit again only if the touch
                 returns.
apparent ladder: the set of places the touch has been, not a set of chosen
                 depths.
```

The honest caveat on this coordinate system: it is a description of **where
consumption was observed**, and it is consistent with — but does not prove —
orders having rested at those prices. Three of four preregistered bars
failed, so it is offered as the **simplest description that fits**, not as a
validated generative rule.
