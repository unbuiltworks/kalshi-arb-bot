## What the descriptors say

Both windows resolved decisively and in **opposite directions** — 1730z to 0.01,
1745z to 0.99 — so neither was a near-tie, and settlement distance from 0.50 is
identical at 0.49. Spreads are identical at one tick. Sweep behaviour, flow
persistence and taker balance are close enough to be unremarkable.

The two descriptors that differ sharply are **net displacement with less path**
versus **more path with no net displacement**:

- 1745z moved **+22.77 bps net** on BTC with a PMUS absolute path of only 7.385.
- 1730z moved **−2.71 bps net** — essentially flat — with a *larger* path of 10.205
  and a wider BTC range.

So 1745z was the more directional window and 1730z the choppier one, despite 1730z
having slightly higher realized volatility. Consistent with that, 1745z built **2.6×
the peak concurrent inventory** and suffered **2.2× the forced-exit share**, and its
baseline lost −108.22 where 1730z's made +59.72.

## The hypothesis this suggests — and why it is not being used

The cancellation signal paid in the directional window that was losing and cost money
in the choppy window that was winning. A mechanistically natural reading is that
cancelling on adverse flow helps when flow is persistently directional and runs a
resting quote over, and hurts when price oscillates and the "adverse" burst is noise
that mean-reverts.

That is a **hypothesis generated by two windows, which is exactly the number that
cannot test it**. It is recorded here and nowhere else — not encoded, not used to
filter, not used to select a ceiling. Confirming it requires untouched pristine
windows, and then validating on a further untouched sample after any rule is written.
