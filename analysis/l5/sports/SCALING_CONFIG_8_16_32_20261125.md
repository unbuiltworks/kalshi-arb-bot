## What each needs and what binds

| | 8 / $10 | 16 / $25 | 32 / $50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resting capital, nothing filled | $7.76 | $15.52 | $31.04 |
| Room left under the account limit for inventory | $2.24 | $9.48 | $18.96 |
| Signed requests a second, today's build | 1.6 (over budget) | 3.2 | 6.2 |
| Signed requests a second, list-based fill detection | 0.38 | 0.55 | 0.88 |
| First cap to bind | account limit, once about two legs are held | campaign unpaired $6 (about 12 one-contract legs) | campaign unpaired $6, then the request budget |
| Limits that must move with it | none beyond the account limit and spend | at-risk budget 16 → 24, lane cap 20 → 25 | at-risk budget → 48, lane cap → 50 |

Points for the decision:

- **None of these fits the request budget on the build now running.** List-
  based fill detection (release candidate, off by default) is what makes
  8 or more markets possible. It has not run live; one thing is still
  unverified — whether the venue's open-order list carries a filled
  quantity on each row. If it does not, the bot falls back to today's
  per-order reads automatically and the request saving does not appear.
  The first locked or one-contract run logs the row's field names once,
  which settles it.
- **The lane cap is a share of a ceiling the crypto lane also reads.**
  Raising it for 16 or 32 markets raises the ceiling the crypto controller
  is given too, unless the split is changed at the same time. The owner's
  rule stands: the change must not increase the crypto controller's
  authorized capacity. This needs its own decision before the $25 stage.
- **32 markets leaves 12% of the request budget spare.** Order sends would
  be deferred in busy periods. 16 is the largest size I would propose on
  the current budget.
- **At $10 with 8 markets the account limit is the binder almost at
  once**: $7.76 is resting, so the third held leg stops new bids. $10
  supports about 6 quoted markets with room to carry inventory, or 8 with
  little.
- **The campaign unpaired cap ($6) becomes the binder at 16 and 32.** With
  it unchanged, at most about 12 one-contract legs can be unpaired across
  all markets however many are quoted. That is a deliberate brake, not a
  defect.
- **Market supply.** At 02:44 UTC 25 Nov the venue listed 80 live events;
  the reward scan of 24 Nov found programmes on most of them. Whether 16
  or 32 of them pass the price band, depth and scope rules at one time
  depends on the hour and on which competitions the owner approves.
  Cricket stays excluded.

## Reward economics — what is known

- Paid and source-accounted: $1.30 for 22 Nov (two Serie A markets, in
  play, two-contract quotes). No reward rows yet for 23–25 Nov.
- Not known: what tonight's one-contract quoting earned. Rows post one to
  two days late; a day under $1 is not paid.
- The venue's scoring rule counts only the price levels needed to reach a
  market's target size. A one-contract bid one tick behind a touch that
  already meets the target scores nothing under the published rule. In
  deep markets (ATP: about 1,400 contracts at the touch) today's quoting
  position therefore has a modelled share near zero; in thin markets the
  level behind the touch still counts. This is the case for ranking by our
  own share (`PMUS_RN1_RANK_SHARE`) and for joining the best bid
  (`PMUS_RN1_JOIN_TOUCH`), both in the release candidate and both off.
- No projection of reward income per configuration is given: there is no
  paid evidence at one contract to base one on.
