# L5 EXTEND and SWITCH: does extending create value, and what predicts a switch

Questions 3 and 4 of the L5 edge decomposition. **RESEARCH ONLY.** Generated by `scripts/l5_extend_and_switch.py`.

> Nothing in this document is realized profit for our account. It describes another trader's public activity.

## Headline

**Q3. Yes - EXTEND_HIGHER precedes the compression that pays, and the deployed 5c improvement gate is contradicted.**

- Right after an EXTEND_HIGHER leg the running pair cost averages **1.0137** - *above par*. By the event's end it is **0.9858**. The change is **-0.0280** [-0.0301, -0.0259] across **4128** independent events: the apparently-bad higher-price fill is followed by compression, not by damage.
- The control is **-0.0059** [-0.0077, -0.0040] (n=4083 ev) after EXTEND_LOWER - compression after a *lower* extend is roughly a fifth as large, so the effect is not a generic end-of-event artifact. The higher extend is where the above-par state gets created and then worked off.
- Events that contain at least one EXTEND_HIGHER end **+0.2869** [0.2624, 0.3110] higher on final matched fraction (4878 vs 847 independent events) than events with actions but no higher extend. Final pair cost is statistically indistinguishable between the two arms, so the higher extend buys *completion*, not a worse price.
- Only **16.8%** of EXTEND_HIGHER legs happen before the event's first matched inventory, so this is mostly re-loading an already paired book at a worse price in order to keep working it, not a reckless opening sequence.
- Settlement P&L cannot adjudicate this: see *Settlement P&L coverage* below. The evidence is pair-cost compression and matched fraction.

**Q4. The strongest predictors of a SWITCH are adverse price movement on the side just bought, and the previous action; inventory imbalance matters much less than expected.**

- Unconditional: EXTEND 60.2%, SWITCH 36.8%, STOP 3.0% over **204599** decision points in **6252** events.
- Recent price movement on the same outcome spans the widest range of P(SWITCH) of any single conditioner: the side moving *up* more than 2c against the next fill raises P(SWITCH) to the top of the table while the side moving *down* more than 2c lowers it, with non-overlapping event-level CIs. L5 switches when the side it just bought has gotten more expensive and extends when it has gotten cheaper.
- Consecutive same-side buys *lower* P(SWITCH) monotonically: a long run predicts more of the same run, not a reversion.
- TTE mostly governs STOP, not SWITCH: inside the last 30 seconds P(STOP) jumps by an order of magnitude while P(SWITCH) falls. The clock ends the sequence rather than redirecting it.
- Inventory imbalance moves P(SWITCH) only a few points, and the fully one-sided state has the *lowest* P(SWITCH) of the inventoried cells - the absence of a pair does not make L5 hurry to complete one.

## Q3 - does EXTEND create value?

The deployed clone (`src/gemini_execution/arming.py`) requires an EXTEND to show >= 5c average improvement. **That gate is not imposed anywhere in this study.** L5's own same-side re-buys go lower and higher at roughly equal rates, so a 5c-improvement requirement describes a minority of L5's extends.

### Q3c - does the higher-price extend PRECEDE the compression?

For each EXTEND_HIGHER leg i in an event: is the event's FIRST matched inventory reached at a leg > i (i.e. does the higher-price extend come BEFORE the pair is completable)? And what is the change in the event's running pair cost from the state right after leg i to the final state? Aggregated to one value per event.

A negative pair_cost_change means the pair cost COMPRESSED after the higher-price extend, i.e. the apparently-bad fill preceded the compression that pays. Positive means it did not.

## Caveats and UNAVAILABLE

- Nothing in this document is realized profit for our account. This is another trader's public activity on Polymarket.
- MAKER SHARE IS UNAVAILABLE. /activity carries no maker/taker liquidity flag, so the maker/taker split of L5's fills cannot be determined and is never estimated here. Because the venue fee schedule is takerOnly, the charged wedge is a LOWER BOUND indicator of taker activity, not a measurement of maker share.
- Fills sharing a timestamp are not time-ordered in the corpus. They are collapsed into size-weighted LEGS, and legs that share a timestamp with the other outcome are marked AMBIGUOUS and excluded from sequential classification (count reported).
- 'Pair cost' here is a charged-cash average cost of held inventory, not a mark-to-market or a modelled fill.
- SETTLEMENT P&L IS EFFECTIVELY UNAVAILABLE for the worked corpus. Only 591 of 6,252 pre-incentive events have a resolved condition in markets.jsonl, and that subset has median 2 legs vs 32 and mean matched fraction 0.26 vs 0.69 - it is the barely-worked tail, not a random sample. The P&L columns are retained as descriptive of that tail and are NOT evidence about EXTEND.
- The deployed clone's 5c EXTEND improvement gate is NOT applied anywhere in this study, and no threshold here is optimized.
- Resolution-dependent P&L is a SETTLEMENT outcome and is reported only as a descriptive consequence; Q4's target is deliberately the next ACTION, never the settlement.
