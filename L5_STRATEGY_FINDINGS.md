## What will be measured, and how

Stating this before the data arrives is deliberate: it fixes the hypotheses and the
decision rules in advance, so the run cannot be quietly reshaped to fit whatever it finds.

| Question | Statistic |
| --- | --- |
| Does size increase at 70c / 80c / 90c? | Spearman(price, shares) pooled; then **within each window**, with a sign test across windows — pooling alone confounds "bigger at high prices" with "bigger in busy windows" |
| 5m vs 15m | Mann-Whitney on ten per-window metrics; two-proportion z on six per-window rates |
| BTC vs ETH | identical battery |
| Cheaper side? | (a) opening fill below 50c, sign test; (b) each buy vs his **own** opposite-side fill within 30s — (b) assumes nothing about the book |
| Speed to the opposite side | distribution of `seconds_to_opposite_side`, bootstrap CI on the median, split by window length |
| Combined pair-cost target | distribution of `up_avg + down_avg` on paired windows; concentration within ±0.005/0.01/0.02/0.05 of the median; share finishing under $1.00 |
| Balanced vs directional | share of windows with residual ≤ 1 share or ≤ 5% of the larger leg |
| Profitable vs losing | same metric battery split on `realizedPnl`, **excluding** rebates and incentives |

Verdicts are capped at "strong evidence" and read "insufficient data" below n=20. No
multiple-comparison correction is applied and dozens of tests are run, so p-values rank
hypotheses — they do not confirm them.

## Candidate behavioural rules — hypotheses awaiting data

Each will be filled in with its support, sample size, counterexample list and confidence
label on the first live run. Right now each is only a hypothesis worth testing, drawn from
the shape of a two-sided crypto Up/Down strategy — **not** from any observation of this
wallet.

| | Rule | Will be confirmed or refuted by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1** | Opens on the side priced below 50c | share of windows whose first fill is under 50c, sign test vs a coin flip |
| **R2** | Crosses to the opposite side shortly after | share of windows trading both sides; median delay with bootstrap CI; slowest windows listed as counterexamples |
| **R3** | Finishes with a matched two-sided book | balanced-finish rate; every unbalanced window listed with its residual |
| **R4** | Accumulates toward a combined pair-cost target | median pair cost, IQR, concentration around the median; windows at or above $1.00 listed |
| **R5** | Buys larger clips as price rises | within-window Spearman sign test; windows with the opposite sign counted |

**Any of these may turn out false.** R3 and R4 in particular have a known confound already
written into the generated report: a tight pair-cost distribution is *consistent with* a
target but is also what mechanically happens if he simply stops buying when the second leg
gets expensive. The report will not claim a target has been demonstrated.

## Implementable pseudocode — parameters unfitted

The generated version of this file substitutes fitted medians for the constants below. As
written, with `UNFITTED` placeholders, this is a shape, not a strategy, and it has not been
validated for profitability by anything.

```python
# Observed-behaviour sketch. Research artifact -- not a trading recommendation.
PAIR_COST_TARGET      = UNFITTED   # median (up_avg + down_avg) across paired windows
OPPOSITE_SIDE_DELAY_S = UNFITTED   # median seconds from first fill to the opposite side
BALANCED_TOLERANCE    = 0.05       # residual as a fraction of the larger leg

def on_window_open(window):
    # R1: open on whichever side is quoted below the midpoint.
    buy(cheaper_side(window), size=base_size(window))

def on_fill(window, state):
    # R2/R4: cross to the other side, then keep adding only while the *combined*
    #        acquisition cost would still leave the matched pair under target.
    other = opposite(state.first_side)
    projected = state.avg_price(state.first_side) + current_price(other)
    if projected <= PAIR_COST_TARGET:
        buy(other, size=size_for_price(current_price(other)))   # R5: size scales with price

def on_window_close(window, state):
    # R3: finish matched -- but the data may well show he does NOT always flatten.
    residual = state.qty(UP) - state.qty(DOWN)
    if abs(residual) > BALANCED_TOLERANCE * max(state.qty(UP), state.qty(DOWN)):
        pass  # left open deliberately: the live run decides whether flattening is real
```

## What this analysis will not be able to tell you, even once populated

- **Order intent.** Only executed fills are public. A window with one side traded may be a
  directional bet, or a two-sided quote whose other leg never filled. These are
  indistinguishable in the data and are never assumed apart.
- **Book context.** Polymarket serves no historical order-book state, and none is inferred.
  "Bought the cheaper side" is measured against the 50c midpoint and against his own
  opposite-side fills — never against quotes we cannot see.
- **True economics.** Liquidity rebates, maker incentives and gas sit outside Polymarket's
  position accounting. A negative realized trading P&L would not show the strategy loses
  money.
- **Causality.** The price/size relationship is observational. Prices rise late in a window
  and sizes may rise late for unrelated reasons; the within-window test reduces but does
  not remove that confound.
- **Anything outside the retrieved period.** Endpoint truncation is recorded in
  `data/l5/cache/fetch_manifest.json` and surfaced in the generated coverage section.
